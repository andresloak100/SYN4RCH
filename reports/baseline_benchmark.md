**Task.** given (population_id, valence, outcome_id, species, acuity, perturbation_class, perturbation_polarity, perturbation_target, sex, diet, challenge), predict direction in {increase, decrease, no_change}

This is direction-of-effect classification, deliberately not quantitative simulation. The baselines below are the simplest predictors capable of answering whether direction is predictable at all; they exist to be the bar that a more sophisticated model must clear before it is worth adopting, and the harness is built to show when the answer is that direction is not predictable from these features.

## 4. Results, one table per grouping

Cell format: `value [95% bootstrap CI] n=<scored rows>/<independent groups>g`. The interval is a percentile cluster bootstrap resampled over **groups**, not rows (1000 replicates, seed 20240101): one lab contributing forty rows about one circuit is forty correlated observations, and a row-level bootstrap would report an interval too narrow by roughly the square root of the average group size. `(!)` marks a number whose n is too small to carry meaning (< 20 scored rows, or < 5 independent groups); it is printed with the warning rather than bare or omitted.

The five families are deliberately **not** combined into one score. Accuracy on a skewed three-class problem mostly measures the skew; macro-F1 answers a different question; ranking and calibration decide whether the outputs can be thresholded or acted on at all. A single headline number would let a model be adopted on the one of the five it happened to win.

The last-but-two column is required beside every cross-validated number by SCHEMA.md 5b: the share of test rows whose **perturbation identity** -- its class, polarity and molecular target -- never appears in its fold's training set. Grouping guarantees that no study crosses a split; it guarantees nothing about whether the held-out perturbation was learnable from the training side. Where that share is high, the accuracy beside it is substantially a measurement of the label prior, and SCHEMA.md 5b is explicit that this is a fact about how the field publishes -- one lab, one gene, one paper -- and not a number to be tuned away.

That column is computed in this harness, against the folds each row's metrics were computed on, from `syn4rch.qc.keys.perturbation_key`. SCHEMA.md 5b names `syn4rch.feasibility.identity` as the module that computes identity reach, so the two notions of identity are checked against each other row by row rather than assumed to match: **agrees on every example; both are built on syn4rch.qc.keys.perturbation_key**.

## 5. Before and after the schema v0.2.0 features

The schema v0.1.0 baselines in this run are **unchanged code** -- the same cell keys, the same backoff order, the same tie rule -- so their rows above are directly comparable with the previous benchmark, and the "before" side of every difference below is that previous measurement recomputed rather than quoted. The features added in v0.2.0 are: `perturbation_class`, `perturbation_polarity`, `perturbation_target`, `sex`, `diet`, `challenge`.

One exception to that comparability, and it is a fold-assignment artefact rather than a change of code: the `record` grouping partitions rows by `record_id`, and `record_id` is a hash of the fields the v0.2.0 migration rewrote. A v0.1.0 baseline's number under `record` therefore differs from the previous benchmark's because the rows landed in different folds, not because the baseline computes anything new. The grouped numbers that matter -- `study_id`, `lab`, `population`, `population_outcome` -- do not depend on record ids and are unchanged.

Each cell is a **paired** difference: both baselines run over the same folds, scored on the rows both answered, with the bootstrap resampling groups of those rows. Two accuracies from the tables above must not be subtracted instead -- the difference of two independent intervals is not an interval for the difference, and these two baselines are strongly correlated because they share a training set and a backoff tail.

`all_rows` and `primary_cell_seen` answer different questions. Where a candidate's primary cell was never populated in training it falls back to the same global mode the floor uses, so those rows are **identical by construction** and can carry no information about the difference; including them dilutes the margin and drags in whatever the label prior happens to be on that subset. `primary_cell_seen` is the subset on which something was actually learned. Both are printed, each labelled, so neither can be quoted as the other.

`verdict` is **established** only when the group-clustered interval and the row-level exact McNemar test agree, **suggestive** when exactly one of them does. On a hundred-odd rows in twenty studies the two routinely disagree, and when they do, the honest reading is the weaker one.

## 6. Does the molecular axis carry a sign? (loss of function vs gain of function)

Schema v0.2.0's claim is that a molecular manipulation has a well-defined sign along its own axis: less of a gene product, or more of it. If that sign reaches the readout, then loss and gain of function on the **same gene** in the same population on the same readout should point opposite ways. This is that test, and it is run at two granularities because they answer different questions.

- Molecular examples: **170**, of which **170** record which gene was manipulated.


| level | what a cell is | cells | cells with both polarities | ...and >= 2 labs | cells mirrored / same / no_change | record pairs opposite / same / no_change | single-study cells m/s/nc | multi-study cells m/s/nc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `by_population_outcome` | gene-pooled: all molecular records sharing a population and a readout, whatever gene was manipulated | 65 | 18 | 15 | 5 / 1 / 12 | 26 / 13 / 130 | 3 / 0 / 0 | 2 / 1 / 12 |
| `by_population_outcome_target` | gene-specific: the same gene in the same population on the same readout, manipulated both ways | 122 | 10 | 0 | 8 / 0 / 2 | 9 / 0 / 8 | 8 / 0 / 2 | 0 / 0 / 0 |

the axis predicts that loss and gain of function on one gene point opposite ways. Mirrored cells and opposite-signed record pairs are evidence for it; 'same' cells and same-signed pairs are evidence against; pairs involving no_change are evidence of neither. At the gene-pooled level a cell with several distinct targets tests nothing about the polarity, because its two sides concern different genes -- and that, rather than the polarity being wrong, is what a negative result there means.

**What this run found.** Hold the gene fixed and the axis does mirror: of the **10** gene-specific cells holding both a loss and a gain of function, **8** have opposite modal directions, **0** have the same, and **2** involve `no_change` on one side; at record level, **9** opposite-signed pairs against **0** same-signed. Pool the genes and that structure disappears: among the gene-pooled cells that draw on more than one study, **2** mirror, **1** are the same and **12** involve `no_change`.

**Why that is not usable as a feature, and the number that says so: 0.** That is how many of those gene-specific cells draw on two or more laboratories. Every one of them is a single study reporting both directions of its own manipulation, which is equally consistent with the polarity carrying a real sign and with a paper reporting a coherent story; nothing here distinguishes the two. And prediction needs more than a sign: it needs the cell to have been **seen in training**. Since each gene in this corpus appears in exactly one study, a leave-one-study-out split removes the only evidence about that gene along with the study, which is why the molecular half of the before/after table above is a column of structural zeros rather than a result. The remedy is not a better model. It is a second laboratory manipulating the same gene in the same population (SCHEMA.md 5b).

### 6.1 What each cell definition does to the data

A finer cell is a smaller cell. In-sample purity is the ceiling an exact-cell lookup could reach if it were scored on the rows it memorised, and it rises **mechanically** as cells shrink: a cell holding one row is trivially pure and contributes nothing to a held-out row of the same cell. Read the two columns together, or the refinement that fragments the data hardest will look like the one that explains it best.

| cell | fields | cells | mean rows per cell | largest cell | singleton cells | in-sample purity (a ceiling, not an accuracy) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `population_outcome` | `population_key`, `outcome_id` | 183 | 2.77 | 62 | 111 (60.7%) | 0.679 |
| `population_axis_outcome` | `population_key`, `perturbation_axis`, `outcome_id` | 306 | 1.66 | 28 | 224 (73.2%) | 0.834 |
| `population_axis_outcome_condition` | `population_key`, `perturbation_axis`, `outcome_id`, `sex`, `diet`, `challenge` | 401 | 1.26 | 13 | 341 (85.0%) | 0.917 |

in_sample_purity is a ceiling measured on the rows it was computed from, not an accuracy; read it against fraction_singleton_cells, because a cell holding one row is trivially pure and contributes nothing to a held-out row.

## 9. The bar a model has to clear

A sophisticated model is not to be adopted in this project unless it beats these numbers **under the same grouping**, with its own group-resampled interval, on at least accuracy and macro-F1 together. Beating `global_majority` on accuracy alone is not evidence of anything: on a skewed label distribution that is a measurement of the skew. Writing the bar down before a candidate exists is the point of this section; a bar set after the candidate's number is known is not a bar.

| grouping | leakage-prone | accuracy to beat (best baseline) | macro-F1 to beat (best baseline) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `study_id` | no | 0.485 [0.444, 0.531] <br>n=507/75g by `perturbation_axis_majority` | 0.480 [0.435, 0.524] <br>n=507/75g by `perturbation_axis_majority` |
| `lab` | no | 0.485 [0.437, 0.531] <br>n=507/68g by `perturbation_axis_majority` | 0.481 [0.433, 0.524] <br>n=507/68g by `perturbation_axis_majority` |

Where a cell says *no number to beat*, the bar is not zero -- it is undefined, because there was not enough data under that grouping to measure anything. A model evaluated there would be equally unmeasurable.


## 10. What these numbers do not say

- **They are not a simulation.** The task is the sign of an effect in three classes. SCHEMA.md 6 refuses to represent dose-response curves, and nothing here predicts a magnitude.
- **They are not an aging result.** Tier B outcomes are excluded from the examples (SCHEMA.md 6). No number here is about lifespan, frailty or healthspan, and no "rejuvenation score" exists in this project.
- **They are conditional on the corpus, not on biology.** A baseline that predicts direction well may be reading a publication habit: the readouts a field reports are the ones that moved, and `no_change` is under-reported at source even though SCHEMA.md 3.5 requires it to be recorded when found.
- **Comparisons across groupings are not comparisons of models.** A number under `record` and a number under `lab` are measurements of different quantities. Only same-grouping comparisons are meaningful.
- **`kg_path_sign` could not be run at all.** The mechanistic baseline(s) that would test whether circuit structure predicts direction are absent from this comparison, so the bar in section 7 is the bar set by frequency and transmitter-sign baselines only, and may be lower than the real one.
- **A margin on one half of a corpus is not a capability.** Section 5 finds a difference on the circuit-activity records and a structural zero on the molecular ones. Nothing here supports a claim about molecular perturbations, and nothing supports extending the circuit-activity result to a population, a readout or a perturbation whose cell has not been seen -- which, per the SCHEMA.md 5b column in section 4, is most of them.
- **"Insufficient evidence to rank these interventions reliably" is still an available answer, and often the correct one.** It is what an abstention is, what an `UNAVAILABLE` baseline is, what a *no number to beat* cell is, and what a slice with no rows is. None of those is a gap to be filled in with a default; filling them in is how a benchmark starts reporting confidence it has not got.
- **Reproducibility.** Every number above is a deterministic function of the dataset, the fold source and the seed printed in section 0. No optional library affects any of them.
